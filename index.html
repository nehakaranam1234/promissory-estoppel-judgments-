RULEBOOK

What is promissory estoppel?

When someone makes a clear promise and the other side acts on it, the law can stop the promise-maker from backing out. In India this idea can apply to the Government as well. The courts have set the rules of the road: the promise must be lawful, and it gives way when a proven public interest requires.

RULES OF THE ROAD

What the Court said, race by race

  • A statute sets the road. A promise cannot create a right that the law does not allow: Augustan Textile Colours (2022), State of Gujarat v. Arcelor Mittal (2022), State of West Bengal v. Gitashree Dutta (2022).
  • The legislature and policy-makers can change lanes. A concession can be changed by law or policy: Union of India v. A.B.P. (2023), Hero Motocorp v. Union of India (2022), Rewa Tollway v. State of Madhya Pradesh (2024).
  • Public interest is the pace car. Puja Ferro Alloys v. State of Goa (2025) and State of Maharashtra v. Reliance Industries (2026) show the Court weighing promises against the wider public interest.
  • Promises can bind the State. CIDCO v. Shishir Realty (2021) and IFGL Refractories v. Orissa State Financial Corporation (2026) are races in which the private party crossed the line first.
  • Incentives are policy. Chowgule & Co. (2022) looks at an export incentive that was part of a policy the Union could revise.
